Wake word — talk hands-free

On desktop Chrome, hands-free listening is ON by default — the ◌ WAKE pill at the bottom of the screen is armed the moment the dashboard loads (it uses the browser's built-in speech recognition, so nothing is sent to us). Tap the pill any time to turn it off; it stays off until you tap it back on. On phones and tablets it defaults off. While it is on, just say any of these and DATA starts taking dictation, no clicking:

  • "Computer" — said on its own
  • "Hey data" · "Hi data" · "Ok data" · "Yo data" (or the same with "computer")
  • "Data, start listening" or "Wake up"
  • Say an officer's name — Vector · Probe · Atlas · Sentinel · Echo — to route the chat to that officer, then speak
  • "Computer, what can you do" — DATA speaks a short rundown of the voice commands (also "computer, help")
  • "Computer, stop" — cut off a spoken reply mid-sentence
  • "Computer, voice off" — switch the wake word off (also "computer, go to sleep"); tap the ◌ WAKE pill to turn it back on
  • "Computer, shut down" — power DATA all the way off (five-second cancel)

Speech-to-text model

What turns your speech into text. Smaller = much faster on a CPU / no-GPU machine (e.g. a Surface). Larger = more accurate. ⤓ = not downloaded yet; picking it installs it. Applies on your next spoken turn.

Recognition speed vs accuracy

Beam size. 1 = greedy = fastest (recommended for slow machines). Higher decodes several ways and ranks them — more accurate, more CPU, slower.

Compute precision

int8 is fastest on a CPU and is the default. The float types only help if you have a GPU.
